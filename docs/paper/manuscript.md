# Label-verified pseudo-alignment and joint registration by recognition for weakly supervised reading of identity-card fields on hand-held photographs

> **Working draft (not submitted).** Numbers marked ◆ come from the scripts in this repository

> references must be checked against publisher records before submission. See `submission-checklist.md`
> for what is still missing for a journal paper.

## Abstract

Reading the national identifier and the dates of an identity card photographed by hand is a recurring
step of remote identity verification, yet systems that segment a line into glyphs fail on imperfectly
rectified photographs, and training line-level recognisers normally needs transcriptions *and* aligned
crops. We study a CPU-only setting in which the only supervision is the line-level text that a
verification database already holds. We propose (i) **label-verified pseudo-alignment**, which places each
training window where a bootstrap recogniser reproduces the known label, so that mis-registered
cards never teach noise, and (ii) **joint registration by recognition**, which estimates the residual
shift and scale of a card by maximising the summed CTC confidence of all fields read from windows tied to
one geometric hypothesis. A 1.1 M-parameter CRNN/CTC model (4 MB, ONNX Runtime, no GPU) is trained with
these two ideas on 1,376 photographs and evaluated on 400 held-out photographs from a public dataset of
fictitious Iranian national ID cards. Compared with a segment-and-classify pipeline, the macro F1 of
three fields rises from ◆0.49 to ◆0.71 and precision of accepted values reaches ◆0.98-0.996, with the CTC
confidence separating correct from incorrect readings (AUC ≈ 1). The method, an operating-point policy
that routes unconfident fields to review, and all code are released; the weights are not, because the
data licence does not permit redistribution.

**Keywords:** identity document recognition; CRNN; connectionist temporal classification; weak
supervision; pseudo-labelling; document registration; KYC.

## 1. Introduction

Remote onboarding (KYC) depends on reading identity-card fields from photographs taken with
consumer devices. Two properties of the setting shape the problem. First, photographs are rectified
from detected card corners that are imprecise, so the text lines land at unpredictable offsets and
scales. Second, production systems hold *labels without geometry*: the national id and the dates are
in a database, but nobody annotated where they are in each image. Resource limits (no GPU, one or two
CPU cores) exclude large transformer recognisers.

Contributions:

1. A recognition protocol that needs only line-level labels (§3.2): label-verified pseudo-alignment.
2. A registration procedure that uses the recogniser itself as the alignment score and ties all fields
   to one hypothesis (§3.3).
3. A confidence-gated decision policy and a quantified precision/recall trade-off for review routing (§5.3).
4. An open, reproducible CPU-only implementation and an honest negative-results log (face-based
   registration, shear search, extra data) (§5.5).

## 2. Related work

*Sequence recognition.* CRNN with CTC [Shi 2017; Graves 2006] is the standard segmentation-free
recogniser; benchmark studies compare scene-text modules [Baek 2019] and transformers [Li 2023 TrOCR]
at much larger scale. We keep the small CRNN because of the CPU budget and show that the gain comes from
supervision and registration, not from architecture.
*Identity-document analysis.* The MIDV family [Arlazarov 2019; Bulatov 2020] provides mobile
video of identity documents; open engines such as Tesseract [Smith 2007] are the usual baseline.
*Pseudo-labelling.* Self-training with model-predicted labels [Lee 2013] is sensitive to wrong labels; our
pseudo-alignment is *verifiable* because the target string is known, which removes label noise by construction.
*Face detection.* YuNet [Wu 2023] is used for orientation checks and portrait extraction (§3.5).

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Pipeline
Decode → card detection (contour scoring over several foreground masks) → perspective rectification
to 1011×638 → orientation (colour/marker classifier, cross-checked by a face detector) → line
recogniser → validation (checksum, calendar) → encrypted retention.

### 3.2 Label-verified pseudo-alignment
Let a window be parameterised by h = (s, dy, dx) relative to the nominal field position. Stage 1
trains a recogniser on nominal windows only. Stage 2 searches, per training card, the nominal window
then a 63-hypothesis grid for an h at which the model reads the *known* label exactly; cards for which
no h verifies are dropped (◆889 of 1,376 are kept). Stage 3 trains the joint model on windows centred at
the verified h with jitter, rotation, shear, photometric noise and 5% empty-target windows.

### 3.3 Joint registration by recognition
At inference, a hypothesis h fixes the window of every field (field offsets from an anchor shrink or grow
with the scale). The score of h is the sum of CTC confidences (mean top-1 probability of emitted characters;
zero if the string is malformed). The best h is chosen; fields below a threshold τ are routed to review.
The nominal hypothesis is tried first (early exit when all fields exceed 0.95).

### 3.4 Network
Four conv blocks (32-64-96-128), vertical position kept in the channel axis, two bidirectional GRU layers
(128), CTC head over 11 symbols + blank. A constant plane encodes the field.

### 3.5 Orientation and portrait
A face found only after a 180° rotation overrules the colour classifier; the portrait is cropped around
YuNet's box and levelled by the eye line.

## 4. Experimental protocol

*Data.* Public Kaggle dataset of fictitious Iranian national ID cards (2,000 hand-held photographs,
labels for id, dates and names). Split by image id (id mod 5 = 0 → 400 test; the rest train). No
hyper-parameter was tuned on the test photographs (τ = 0.9 fixed a priori). **Caveats:** the cards and faces
are synthetic, many photographs share a face, and most ids fail the official checksum, so the checksum was
disabled in evaluation.
*Metrics.* Over all test photographs (a rejected photograph is a miss): accuracy, precision among
auto-accepted values, recall (exact and auto-accepted over all), F1; 95% percentile bootstrap over
photographs (2,000 draws); exact McNemar tests on paired exact matches; paired bootstrap of ΔF1.
*Baselines.* (a) Tesseract digits/Persian; (b) segment-and-classify (connected components, HOG, k-NN trained on the
same real glyphs); (c) proposed.
*Ablations.* nominal window only; independent per-field search; joint search; no pseudo-alignment; seeds.

## 5. Results

### 5.1 Main comparison (400 test photographs, full pipeline) ◆

| Field | Tesseract F1 | Glyph k-NN F1 [95% CI] | Proposed F1 [95% CI] | Proposed precision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| National id | 0.135 | 0.468 [0.416, 0.519] | **0.723 [0.681, 0.762]** | 0.996 |
| Birth date | 0.273 | 0.498 [0.444, 0.551] | **0.704 [0.660, 0.745]** | 0.978 |
| Expiry date | 0.198 | 0.494 [0.440, 0.547] | **0.687 [0.643, 0.731]** | 0.986 |

Paired tests, proposed vs glyph k-NN (exact match): id 60 vs 0 discordant, McNemar p = 1.7e-18; birth date
83 vs 0, p = 2.1e-25; expiry 78 vs 0, p = 6.6e-24; ΔF1 95% CIs [+0.22, +0.29], [+0.16, +0.25],
[+0.15, +0.23]. Glyph k-NN vs Tesseract: all p < 1e-17.

### 5.2 Search ablation (325 test cards that reach OCR, τ = 0.9) ◆

| Strategy | id F1 | birth F1 | expiry F1 | macro F1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Nominal window | 0.708 | 0.708 | 0.671 | 0.696 |
| Independent per-field search | 0.797 | 0.656 | 0.784 | 0.746 |
| Joint registration (proposed) | 0.788 | 0.777 | 0.765 | 0.777 |

Joint registration mainly fixes the *date* fields, which independent search confuses with the neighbouring
date line (birth-date precision 0.81 → 0.98).

### 5.3 Confidence as a decision signal
AUC of the CTC confidence for correctness is ≥ 0.99 for all fields under the proposed search ◆. (Precision/

### 5.4 Seeds and pseudo-alignment ablation
Three seeds of the joint stage (same bootstrap and alignment), joint search, 325 test cards, τ = 0.9 ◆:

| Run | id F1 | birth F1 | expiry F1 | macro F1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| seed 1 (reported) | 0.788 | 0.777 | 0.765 | 0.777 |
| seed 2 | 0.787 | 0.771 | 0.774 | 0.777 |
| seed 3 | 0.788 | 0.786 | 0.780 | 0.785 |
| no pseudo-alignment (seed 4) | 0.764 | 0.733 | 0.746 | 0.748 |

Macro F1 over the three seeds: 0.780 ± 0.005 (mean ± sample std, n = 3). Removing the label-verified pseudo-alignment (every card trained at the nominal window, one run) lowers macro F1 by 0.032, more than the whole spread of the three seeds (0.008), but from a single run; the bootstrap and alignment stages were *not* re-seeded, so the reported variance understates the full training variance.

### 5.5 Negative results ◆
Face-based registration (warping to a median face box): id accuracy 41.8% → 36.8%. Shear search on the digit
line: breaks the slanted date separator. Adding the 144 late-arriving photographs (1,856 → 2,000): ≈ +1 point.

## 6. Limitations
Fictitious, single-design, single-source data; one train/test split; thresholds not validated on a second
dataset; names (Persian script) are not solved (≈25% exact, always reviewed); 45% of test photographs
receive no confident reading (rejected card, strong perspective, blur); checksum not exercised.
No evaluation on real cards (consent and legal basis required).

## 7. Ethics and data
No real person's data was used. Any real-card evaluation requires consent, a lawful basis and
the retention controls described in `docs/privacy-and-security.md`. Face crops are biometric-adjacent data.

## 8. Conclusion
Line-level labels suffice to train an accurate, calibrated, CPU-only reader when training windows are
verified against the label and inference registers the card by recognition.
